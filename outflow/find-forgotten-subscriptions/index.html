How to find the subscriptions you've forgotten about

Almost nobody knows what they actually spend on subscriptions. Not because people are careless — because subscriptions are designed to be forgettable. They are small, they are automatic, and nothing ever asks you to confirm them again.

~9 in 10underestimate their own subscription spending
2 in 5have forgotten one entirely while still paying
8–12subscriptions per household — about 4 actually used
7 in 10have missed cancelling a free trial in time

Figures from 2026 consumer surveys on subscription spending. They are consistent across sources on the direction, less so on the exact amount — which is rather the point.

The good news: you do not need to hand your bank login to anyone to fix this. Forgotten subscriptions hide in four places, and you can check all of them in about ten minutes. Here is the whole method.

1. Apple Subscriptions — start here

1On iPhone or iPad: Settings → tap your name at the top → Subscriptions.

This is the highest-yield sixty seconds you will spend. Everything billed through the App Store lives in one list, including trials that have already converted.

The thing most people get wrong: deleting an app does not cancel its subscription. The billing agreement is with Apple, not with the app, so the charges carry on to an app that is no longer on your phone. This is the single most expensive misconception in the whole category.

2. Ninety days of bank and card statements

2Download the last three months from each account and card. Three months matters: quarterly and annual renewals will not appear in a single month.

Pay closest attention to small amounts. Charges under about £25 are the ones people's eyes slide over, and they are exactly where forgotten subscriptions live. A £4.99 line item you cannot place is worth £60 a year.

Watch for merchant names that do not match the product — billing descriptors are frequently a parent company you have never heard of.

3. Your email receipts

3Search your mail for "receipt", "renewal" and "your subscription", restricted to the last 90 days.

This catches the things that never touch a card statement you still have — subscriptions paid through PayPal, or on a card you have since replaced. A free trial that converted almost always left a receipt behind.

4. The ones people actually forget

4Recognition is far easier than recall, which is why this step finds things the other three miss. Read the list and tick anything you still pay for:

  • Delivery memberships — Amazon Prime, Deliveroo Plus, Uber One, recipe boxes
  • Software trials that converted — Adobe, Microsoft 365, Dropbox, Google One, a VPN, a password manager
  • Fitness — bought in January, forgotten by March: Peloton, ClassPass, Strava, meditation apps
  • Streaming beyond the obvious — the second and third services, not Netflix
  • News and creators — a paper, a Substack, a Patreon, small amounts that never feel like subscriptions

Then write it all down in one place

The audit is only worth anything if the list survives it. Whatever you use — a notes app, a spreadsheet, paper — get every subscription, its price and its renewal date into one place. Otherwise you will do this again next year from scratch.

Common questions

Do I need to link my bank account?

No. Bank linking automates detection, and for some people that convenience is worth it. But it means your entire transaction history — salary, rent, medical payments — flows through a third-party aggregator. The manual method above finds the same charges. Outflow's middle path is reading a statement you hand it, on your device, with no credentials involved.

How often should I do this?

Once a year is plenty. Subscriptions accumulate slowly, and a monthly audit is the kind of chore nobody sustains. Put it next to something you already do annually — renewing insurance, or filing a tax return.

What about subscriptions on a card I've cancelled?

These are the sneakiest, because they vanish from your statements while sometimes still being collected via a replacement card or PayPal. The email-receipt step is the one that catches them.
